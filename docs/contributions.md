# Contributions

Who made this library, and what each part of it rests on.

`data/contributions.json` lists every contribution the repository already
records, by contributor. It is generated by `scripts/build_contributions.py`
from the files that say who did what, and it adds no field anybody has to
fill in. Edit the records, never the output.

## What counts

| Kind | Who | Read from |
|---|---|---|
| `wrote` | The authors of every record the library holds | each record's byline |
| `proposed` | Whoever proposed a record | the record's provenance (`from issue #N by @login`) |
| `reviewed-statement` | Whoever judged whether a statement says what its paper says | `evals/gold/claims.yml` |
| `reviewed-filing` | Whoever decided where a record belongs in the taxonomy | `evals/gold/tags.yml` |
| `asserted-relation` | Whoever judged how two statements relate | `data/claim-relations.yml` |
| `verified-identity` | The maintainer who confirmed who a reviewer is | `data/people/` |
| `drafted` | The model that drafted a statement *(machine)* | each statement's provenance |
| `admitted` | Citation expansion, admitting a record the corpus cites *(machine)* | each record's provenance |

**Authors come first** because everything here depends on them. A statement
is drawn from somebody's paper, a verdict judges that statement, and a
relation joins two of them. Each contribution records what it `builds_on`,
so credit can be followed back to the paper and the people who wrote it.

**One person, one entry.** A reviewer whose GitHub account is a verified link
to a name on a byline is listed under that name, so an author who reviewed
statements from their own paper appears once, as both. Their verdicts are
marked `by_author`. An unverified login stays a login (`@name`), because
joining two identities on a guess is worse than leaving them apart.

## How it is used, recomputed

A contribution goes on being used after it is made. A paper is cited by
records admitted later, statements are drawn from it, and relations join
those statements to others. `used_by` records that, and it is recomputed on
every build, so it grows with the library rather than freezing at the moment
of contribution.

It is a count of use, not a measure of worth. A citation can be a criticism,
and a relation can be a disagreement.

## What is deliberately absent

- **No scores, weights or ranking.** People are listed by name, never by
  count, because an order by count is a ranking and a ranking is a weighting
  nobody has agreed to. If the library ever values contributions, the
  valuation will be proposed in the open, where the people it values can see
  and contest it, before it is built.
- **Machine contributions are not a person's.** A model's drafts and the
  citation crawl's admissions are listed apart, under `machines`. They are
  real work the library rests on, and they are not judgment. Machines admit,
  people judge.
- **Code and documentation** are not counted here. Git already records who
  wrote them, and a second record would only disagree with it.

## Where this comes from

The design follows four of the nine principles in Rennie and Potts, *Design
Principles for Contribution Systems* (SSRN 5190625), chosen for a library
this small:

- **Value is created through networks of dependencies.** Here this is
  `builds_on`, and authors come first.
- **Dynamic and continuous valuation.** Here this is `used_by`, recomputed on
  every build.
- **Legibility in value recognition.** Every entry names the file it was read
  from, and nothing is hidden or weighted. Their account of SourceCred is the
  warning: it became a problem when contributors could no longer see how they
  were weighted.
- **Machine-readable contributions, with human intervention.** The ledger is
  data, and machine work is kept apart from human judgment.

Three of the other five are about rewarding contributors and growing a
contributor base: prioritizing high-value contributors, rewarding contributors
over passive participants, and reaching critical mass. This library rewards
nobody, so it adopts none of them. It records and credits. If it ever
rewards, those principles are where to start.

## Correcting it

If you are missing, miscounted, or listed under two names, open an issue.
The fix is almost always in the record the entry was read from: a byline, a
provenance line, or an identity link in `data/people/`.
